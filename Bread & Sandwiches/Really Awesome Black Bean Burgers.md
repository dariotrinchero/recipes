# Ingredients

- 2 (15-ounce) cans black beans, rinsed and drained
- 4 tablespoons vegetable oil, divided
- 3 medium onions, one finely chopped (about 1 cup) and the other two sliced for caramelisation
- 1 large red pepper, finely chopped (about 1 cup)
- 3 medium cloves garlic, minced
- 1 large jalapeno chilli, finely chopped, plus 1 teaspoon smoky hot sauce
- 3/4 cup roasted cashews
- 1 tablespoon white miso (in place of feta)
- Squeeze of lemon juice
- 3/4 cup panko bread crumbs
- 3 tablespoons chickpea flour (binder, in place of egg; see Notes)
- 1 tablespoon vital wheat gluten (binder)
- 2 tablespoons [[Vegan Mayonnaise]]
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- Vegan cheese for topping, such as [[Vegan Mozzarella]] or Fauxmage (optional)
- 6 to 8 hearty hamburger buns (check they're egg- and dairy-free), toasted
- Condiments as desired, such as chipotle vegan mayonnaise, ketchup, or mustard
- Toppings as desired, such as shredded lettuce, sliced onions, and pickles

# Method

1. Adjust oven rack to centre position and preheat oven to 350°F (175°C). Spread black beans in a single layer on a foil-lined rimmed baking sheet. Roast until beans are mostly split open and outer skins are beginning to get crunchy, about 20 minutes. Remove from oven and allow to cool slightly.
2. While beans roast, heat 2 tablespoons oil in a medium skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering. Add onion and pepper and cook, stirring frequently, until softened, about 5 minutes. Add garlic and cook, stirring constantly, until fragrant, about 2 minutes. Add chilli and sauce and cook, stirring, until fragrant, about 30 seconds. Transfer mixture to a large bowl.
3. Caramelise the remaining two onions in some oil.
4. Place cashews in the bowl of a food processor and pulse until chopped into pieces no larger than 1/3-inch, about 12 short pulses. Add to bowl with onions and peppers.
5. When beans are slightly cooled, transfer to food processor. Pulse until beans are roughly chopped (the largest pieces should be about 1/3 of a full bean in size). Transfer to bowl with onion/pepper mixture. Add caramelised onions. Whisk the miso and lemon juice into the vegan mayonnaise, then add to the bowl along with the bread crumbs. Sprinkle over the chickpea flour and vital wheat gluten, and season with salt and pepper (go easy on salt; the miso is salty). Mix thoroughly with hands, a little more vigorously than you would with egg, to help the gluten develop. Rest the mixture in the fridge for at least 30 minutes so the flours hydrate and the mixture firms up. Patty mixture can be stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to three days at this stage.
6. **To cook indoors:** Form bean mixture into 6 to 8 patties as wide as your burger buns. Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a large nonstick or cast iron skillet over medium heat until shimmering. Add 4 patties and cook, swirling pan occasionally, until well browned and crisp on first side, about 5 minutes. Carefully flip and cook until second side is browned, about 5 minutes longer, adding vegan cheese if desired. If cooking more than 4 burgers, cook in batches, keeping cooked burgers on a rack set in a rimmed baking sheet in a 200°F oven while second batch cooks.
7. Spread top and bottom buns with chipotle vegan mayonnaise or other condiments as desired. Add toppings to top or bottom bun as desired. Place patties on bottom buns, close burgers, and serve immediately.

# Notes

- Caramelised onions are my own modification to the original recipe. They significantly improve the flavour and structural integrity.
- **Veganised (untested).** The binder is a best guess: chickpea flour sets when cooked much like egg, and a little vital wheat gluten adds chew and cohesion. The miso & lemon replace the feta's salty, tangy pockets. If the patties are too soft, chill them (formed) for another 30 minutes before frying.
- Binder alternatives to try:
    - **Methylcellulose** (the binder used in commercial plant-based burgers; not yet tried): use roughly 1.5-2% of the mixture's weight (~10-15g for this batch), hydrated in a few tablespoons of cold water until it gels, in place of the chickpea flour and gluten. It binds strongly and firms up as it heats.
    - **More vital wheat gluten** (up to ~3 tbsp) for a firmer, chewier, more "meaty" patty; too much becomes rubbery.
    - **Chickpea flour only** (~4-5 tbsp) for a gluten-free version, at the cost of some cohesion.
    - Flax eggs and aquafaba bind patties poorly; avoid.
