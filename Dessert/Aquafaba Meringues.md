# Ingredients

- 180ml (3/4 cup) aquafaba, from one 400g tin of low-sodium chickpeas, at room temperature
- 1/4tsp cream of tartar
- 100g (1/2 cup) sugar
- 1tsp vanilla extract

# Method

1. Preheat the oven to 95°C, with racks in the upper-middle and lower-middle positions, and line 2 baking trays with parchment.
2. Combine the aquafaba and cream of tartar in the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the whisk attachment, and beat on medium until foamy, 2-3min.
3. Still on medium, pour in the sugar gradually, followed by the vanilla. Increase to high, and beat to stiff peaks, about 5min more.
4. Scoop the meringue into a piping bag fitted with a 13mm (#806) plain tip, and pipe 2.5cm rounds onto the trays — about 48 per tray.
5. Bake until dry and crisp, about 1.5h.
6. Transfer the trays to wire racks, and cool the meringues completely before serving.

# Notes

- Any trace of fat will stop the aquafaba whipping, so start with a scrupulously clean bowl and whisk.
- Use the liquid from low-sodium chickpeas — regular tins can carry enough salt to taste in so delicate a sweet. Save the chickpeas for [[Extra Smooth Hummus]] or [[Confit Tandoori Chickpeas]].
- Aquafaba also stands in for egg white in [[Vegan Mayonnaise]].
- Keeps up to a week in an airtight container; they go sticky if left out in humid weather.
- *Mocha variation*: in step 3, add 2tsp instant espresso powder and 6 drops chocolate bitters after the sugar and vanilla. Optionally dip the cooled bases in tempered chocolate and leave to set.
- *Strawberry & vanilla bean variation*: reduce the vanilla extract to 1/2tsp, and rub the seeds of 1/2 vanilla pod into the sugar before using it. Fold 1Tbsp (4g) finely ground freeze-dried strawberries into the finished meringue before piping. Optionally dip the cooled bases in tempered white chocolate.
