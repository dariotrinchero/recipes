# Ingredients

- 4 x 400g large floury potatoes
- 3/4 tsp cooking salt / kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper
- 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
- 3 tbsp / 50g vegan block butter (unsalted if possible), cut into 1cm cubes
- 1 tsp soy milk powder (to mimic brown butter; see Notes)
- 6+ thyme sprigs (sub 1/2 tsp dried thyme)
- 1 cup vegetable stock

# Method

1. **Preheat** oven to 200°C (180°C fan-forced).
2. **Carve** each potato into cylinders ~6cm diameter, 3.5cm height. One can be more or less precise about this, depending on desired presentation.
3. **Season –** Pat potato dry. Place in a large bowl and toss with half the oil plus all the salt and pepper.
4. **Sear –** In an ovenproof heavy based skillet, heat the remaining 1 tbsp oil over medium high heat. Put the potato in and cook each side for 6 – 8 minutes or until golden.
5. **Brown & baste –** Add the butter and sprinkle over the soy milk powder, swirling the pan so the powder disperses in the melting butter. Let it foam and cook for 1-2 minutes until the solids turn nutty brown (watch closely; it can scorch quickly). Add thyme and spoon the butter over the potato ("basting"). Add stock, bring to a boil then transfer to the oven and bake for 30 minutes until the potatoes are tender, basting at the 15 minute mark. The stock will be absorbed by the potato, leaving just butter.

# Notes

- **Veganised from the original (untested).** The original used dairy butter. The golden crust comes from the oil sear in step 4, so it is unaffected; but the nutty brown-butter flavour comes from milk proteins & sugars browning, which most vegan block butters lack. The soy milk powder supplies proteins & sugars that brown similarly – this is a best guess, so adjust the quantity once tested.
- Alternatives for the brown-butter note:
    - A splash (~1 tbsp) of soy milk instead of the powder; cook until the water evaporates and the solids brown.
    - A cultured cashew-based butter (e.g. Miyoko's), which contains some protein and browns somewhat on its own.
    - ~1 tsp white miso mashed into the butter; a different flavour, but it browns and adds a similar savoury depth.
