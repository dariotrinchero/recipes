# Ingredients

- 700g eggplant, cubed
- 450g cherry tomatoes, halved
- 1 yellow onion, chopped
- 2 sticks celery, diced
- 50g (1/3 cup) green olives, pitted & chopped
- 2Tbsp capers, rinsed
- 2Tbsp tomato paste
- ~40ml (a shot) white wine vinegar
- 1Tbsp sugar
- sunflower oil, for frying
- olive oil
- salt & pepper, to taste
- fresh basil, to finish

# Method

1. Heat a generous layer of sunflower oil in a large pan, and fry the cubed eggplant in batches until golden brown and tender. Drain each batch on paper towels.
2. In a separate deep skillet, sweat the celery and onion in a drizzle of olive oil until soft and translucent.
3. Stir in the tomato paste, and cook for about 1min.
4. Add the cherry tomatoes, olives, capers, vinegar, sugar, and a pinch each of salt & pepper. Simmer for 5min, until the tomatoes have broken down a little.
5. Fold the fried eggplant and the basil back into the pan, stirring gently so as not to mash the eggplant.
6. Remove from the heat and leave to rest. Caponata is traditionally served at room temperature or cold, never hot.

# Notes

- The *agrodolce* (sweet & sour) balance of vinegar against sugar is the point of the dish; taste and adjust it once the caponata has cooled, as both flavours soften as it rests.
- It is markedly better on the second day. Keeps in a jar in the fridge for about a week.
- Fry the eggplant in batches in plenty of oil, in a single layer: crowded eggplant steams, and soaks up oil without browning.
- Good alongside bread, or with [[Butternut Risotto & Arancini]]. For a very different eggplant dish, see [[Eggplant in Brown Sauce (Hong Shao Qie Zi)]].
