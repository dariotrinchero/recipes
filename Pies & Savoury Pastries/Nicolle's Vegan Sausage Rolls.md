# Ingredients

- 500g brown mushrooms, finely (5-6mm) brunoise diced
- 3 medium aubergines
- 2 medium onions, finely diced
- 5 cloves garlic, crushed
- 1Tbsp Italian herbs
- 1Tbsp paprika
- 300ml white wine
- Beef stock cube (Knorr)
- 1 can black beans, drained and rough diced
- 2Tbsp soy sauce
- Vinegar, to taste
- 2 rolls vegan puff pastry (I used PnP brand)

# Method

1. Add mushrooms to cold pot in a little oil, and sweat out over medium high heat.
2. Place aubergines underneath broiler and broil for 8-10 min on each side, until burnt skin forms, but without charring the flesh. You can also do this on a gas flame/fire. Once charred, remove and wrap in foil to sweat out.
3. Remove mushrooms from pan. Add oil, and saute the onions on low, adding a little salt and MSG. Let the onions begin browning and caramelising. Deglaze with beef stock and white wine.
4. Skin and dice the aubergines finely, roughly the same size as the mushrooms.
5. Once the onions are browned and sticky, shove them to the sides of the pan, add some oil in the middle, and saute garlic until fragrant. Add the aubergine and mushrooms, and let them get to know each other with a little more white wine and soy sauce. Add Italian herbs and paprika.
6. Add the diced beans and continue to cook on low, stirring intermittently as the beans can make things burn quickly. Deglaze with wine and add salt, MSG, and a little vinegar to taste. Cook down until the mixture is “sticky” and leaves an open path that does not budge when you drag your spoon across the bottom.
7. Let this cool down to room temp, and spoon in a line along the centre of the defrosted pastry (typically I bisect a single sheet of puff pastry lengthwise into two strips), folding it over and pressing the tines of a fork down on the edges to seal them. Leave enough of a gap! Cut each roll into 6-8 pieces, depending on desired size.
8. Array rolls on a baking sheet, brush over the top with a little olive oil, sprinkle with sesame seeds, and bake as per the pastry instructions.

# Notes

- Adding some tomato paste near the end is also a great idea.
